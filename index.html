The Proposition // Setup

What are we looking at?

One line of orientation, so the analysis knows the context it's critiquing.

01
Hypothesis // Market

Who, exactly?

Get specific. Down to the individual. Personas-by-committee fail this card. The buyer should be a real human you could put a face to.

Title, company size, geography. The exact human who pulls out the credit card or signs the PO.
What's the concrete, painful, recurring problem this individual has? Not a category — a moment.
The three real reasons humans buy. Pick the dominant one, then say why.
Laidstatus, belonging, attraction
Madeachievement, mastery, advancement
Paidmoney, security, savings
02
Hypothesis // Product

What value lands, and how fast?

The promise you make in marketing has to land inside the product. The faster, the better. A long Time to Value is the most common cause of cascading failure.

The actual value delivered. The promise you'd put on the homepage.
What pulls them in? Can it form a new habit, or replace an existing one?
The single moment a user feels the value. Sean Ellis: must-have, to whom, why. If you can't name it precisely, that's the first red flag.
How fast from sign-up to Aha. Minutes, hours, days, weeks, months.
Why do they come back tomorrow? Network effect, data lock-in, habit, switching cost.
Onboarding's only job is to drive users to the Aha. What's the path? What gets stripped to make it shorter?
The marketing promise must be backed by what's in the product today — not what's on the roadmap. List the features that deliver the promise. Where's the gap?

Hypothesis // Model

The model is the bet.

Steve Blank: a startup is a temporary entity set up to find a scalable, repeatable business model. Don't dial this in. Strategyzer catalogues dozens of patterns — sometimes the model IS the innovation (Apple's telco-subsidised handset; Xerox per-page).

Mouse$100 ARPA · ~1M customers · viral
Rabbit$1k ARPA · ~100k · self-serve
Deer$10k ARPA · ~10k · inside sales
Elephant$100k ARPA · ~1k · field sales
Whale$1M ARPA · ~100 · enterprise
Pick the closest, or describe a hybrid: subscription, freemium, transactional, marketplace, multi-sided platform, razor-and-blade, pay-per-use, revenue share, aggregator, open-source-with-paid-services, white-label, franchise, long tail.
Some companies' biggest innovation is the model, not the product. If you're doing something non-obvious here (subsidies, unbundling, new pricing axis, who-pays inversion), say it. If not, say "standard pattern" — that's also valid.
How many customers at this ARPA to hit your target ARR? Is that number realistic given the animal you picked?
The absolute floor. If ARPU doesn't cover CAC over a reasonable payback window, nothing else matters. Be explicit about both numbers.
